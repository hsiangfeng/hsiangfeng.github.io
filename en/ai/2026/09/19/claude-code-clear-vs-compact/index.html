30 Days of Vibe Coding | Day 6: Context and Tokens — Why AI Gets Dumber the Longer You Chat

Intro

Once you start using AI to actually get things done, weird stuff starts happening. You clearly gave it some rules, but it seems to forget them, answers something you didn't ask, or even changes something it already fixed back to the broken version.

Most people's first instinct when this happens is...

"Did the AI just get dumber?" "Is the AI company secretly turning down the model's IQ?"

But a lot of the time, the real problem is that the Context inside the Context Window is full, and the AI can't work properly anymore.

And you can't really talk about Context without talking about Tokens, since the two are tightly connected.

Once you get this, you'll understand why AI gets dumber the longer you chat, and why people who use AI a lot keep telling you to open a new conversation for each task.

Tokens: the building blocks of the AI world

Before we get to Context, let's meet the most important thing in the AI world: the Token. It's also the unit AI companies bill you in.

After the last few days of hands-on work, you're probably used to the "send a Prompt => AI replies" loop by now. But have you ever wondered how the AI actually "reads" what you type?

AI doesn't read word by word like we do. It first chops your text into small chunks and then does its math on those chunks. Each chunk is called a Token.

Think of Tokens as LEGO bricks. Whatever you send the AI gets broken down into individual little bricks. Once the AI has the bricks, it thinks about which bricks you probably want to come next, then snaps them together one by one and hands them back to you.

Say you send the AI this:

Text
Hello there!

The AI would break it into something like:

  • "Hello"
  • " there"
  • "!" — three bricks

Then, based on those three bricks, it predicts which bricks you probably want next.

Note

Different models may split Tokens differently, but they generally split by word or piece of a word.

You can also picture the AI sitting on a huge pile of bricks that are sorted into bins, like...

  • A bin for everyday conversation bricks
  • A bin for code bricks
  • A bin for bricks related to all kinds of file formats

And so on. It compares everything in those bins against the bricks you handed it. That comparing process is what we call "compute". Once it's done comparing, it snaps the result into new bricks and hands them back to you.

How AI snaps Tokens together into a reply for you

So for this section, just remember two things:

  • The more text you send, the more Tokens there are, and the more compute the AI has to burn.
  • It's not only your text that counts. Every word the AI replies with is a Token too.

Remember how I said at the start that "Tokens are also the billing unit"? That means every back-and-forth with the AI burns Tokens, and AI companies charge you based on how many you use.

That's also why beginners are generally told not to jump straight into pay-as-you-go API billing. You have no real sense of how many Tokens you'll burn, and one slip and your bill explodes (time to sell a kidney). For most people, a subscription plan is more than enough.

So what do Tokens have to do with AI getting dumber over time? Hang on, that's where our next main character comes in: the Context Window.

Context Window: a table with a size limit

Now that we've covered Tokens, let's meet the Context Window. In short, it's the total limit on everything the AI can "see" at once.

That's probably a bit abstract, so let me use a table as an example.

Say the Context Window is a table, and it's a 100 x 100 cm table (like the 100k Tokens Claude Code labels). Every sentence you say to the AI, every file it reads in, every command output, all of that is stuff sitting on the tabletop.

A table with a size limit

Whenever you talk to the AI, it first looks over everything on the table and then replies. It's like chatting with a friend, except this friend, after hearing your question, glances over the table, figures out what you want and how to answer, writes the answer down on a sheet of paper, puts that sheet on the table, and only then replies to you.

"Why does the AI write it on paper and put it back on the table?"

Just picture the AI as a friend with a really bad memory. If it doesn't write down what it's about to say, it forgets what it was going to say and starts making stuff up.

As you ask more and more questions, the table obviously gets more and more crowded. And then you hit a problem...

There's almost no room left on the table for anything new!

When the table is about to run out of space, the AI takes it upon itself to tidy up. While doing that, it picks out what it thinks is important, condenses it into a cheat sheet, and throws the rest away. That process is called "Auto compact".

And you have zero control over Auto compact. You can't know what it'll throw away and what it'll keep. That's exactly why the AI seems to forget rules you gave it, answer something you didn't ask, or undo things it already fixed.

So if you want to get along with AI long term, learning to manage this table is part of your daily routine (kind of like opening a million Chrome tabs and never closing any). I'll show you how to manage it later on.

The truth behind "the longer you chat, the dumber it gets"

Once you understand Tokens and the Context Window, you'll see that "the AI gets dumber over time" isn't mysterious at all.

Picture this: you've been chatting with the AI all afternoon. What does your table (the Context Window) look like? It's covered in all the notes the AI wrote down after each of your Prompts, like:

  • Your conversation history with the AI
  • File contents the AI read in
  • Output from commands the AI ran

So when the table is almost full, every AI Agent does the same thing: it helpfully condenses the earlier conversation into a summary and throws the rest away to make room for new stuff. And that compression is "lossy", meaning details get lost.

Say the rule you gave the AI at the very beginning was:

Please reply to me in Traditional Chinese.

When the AI starts clearing the table (compressing the conversation), it may decide this isn't a big deal and drop the rule during compression. And from then on, it starts replying in English.

/context: see how messy your table is right now

Now let's look at a real example. Grab the sample files from GitHub #day-6, go into claude-playground, start Claude Code, and type /context in the input box. This command shows you how much of your table is taken up right now, like...

  • How much the System Prompt takes
  • How much System Tools take
  • How much Skills take

The /context visualization after typing the command, showing how much each block takes up

Note

The Context you see on your machine may look different from my screenshot above. Everyone's conversation is different, and so are the Skills and tools loaded by default, so there'll be some variation.

Early on, I'd recommend getting into the habit of checking context, because seeing how full the table is with your own eyes is way more convincing than any article. Here's what it looks like right after starting up, before doing anything:

/context right at the start of a conversation, with the table basically empty

Next, let's run a little experiment so you can watch Context fill up:

Text
Read index.html, then explain line by line what each part of the code does. Then give me five suggestions for improving this page, and finally paste the full improved code right here in the chat for me to see. Don't touch any files yet.

This Prompt makes the AI do three table-hogging things in one go: read the whole index.html (file contents land on the table), output a long line-by-line explanation (every word it replies with is a Token), and then paste the whole file back to you again (another big stack of paper on the table).

Once it's done replying, type /context again, and you'll see the table now looks like this:

/context after heavy usage, compared with the previous screenshot

You can see it jumped from 29.3k to 49.5k, and messages went from one block to five. That's what your table looks like after chatting with the AI for a while.

/clear vs /compact: a new table vs tidying the table

After reading all that, you're probably thinking:

"If I don't want Auto compact to kick in, is there another way to deal with this?"

There is. So here are two commands you really need to know when building with AI: /clear and /compact.

First up is /clear (or /new). The idea is simple: throw everything on the table away and start over.

Note

I say "throw everything away," but a later post will cover how to get the AI back to its original table setup every time you clear.

/compact is pretty similar to Auto compact, except you can add instructions about "what to keep". What does that mean? Basically, with Auto compact the AI decides on its own what's important. With /compact, you get to say what's important. For example, the AI might think "please reply in Traditional Chinese" doesn't matter, but with /compact you can tell it that's a key point it can't drop, so it keeps it when tidying the table.

Using it is really simple:

Text
/compact Keep the requirements for the current feature and any unresolved issues, everything else can go

That way, when the AI compresses, it knows exactly what matters and what can be tossed.

I do want to point out that /compact isn't magic. In practice, I'd recommend running /compact when Context is around 60–70% full. Tidying up takes table space too, so if you wait until Context is at 90% before tidying, there's a good chance you'll lose something you consider important.

Both commands have their own timing and use cases, so here's a chart for reference:

Chart for reference

So my personal rule of thumb is:

  • Once a task is done, get a new table (/clear).
  • If the Context Window hits around 60–70%, tidy the table first (/compact), then keep going on the same task.

This kind of control is pretty much enough to keep you out of the "dumber the longer you chat" trap. Combine it with using /compact at the right time, and you can hold on to the key points you care about within the same task.

Note

Just to be clear: if the AI gets dumber because the AI company is short on compute, that's a totally different thing from a full Context Window. The point here is that a full Context Window makes the AI dumber, not a lack of compute. If you want to track whether AI models are actually getting dumber, check out my earlier post Is AI Getting Quietly Dumber? AIStupidLevel: A 24-Hour Watchdog for LLM Degradation. It might help.

Try watching the Context Window change

Let's go back to claude-playground for a small experiment.

If the conversation from the /context section is still open, just keep using it. If you already closed it, restart Claude Code and type this again:

Text
Read index.html, then explain line by line what each part of the code does. Then give me five suggestions for improving this page, and finally paste the full improved code right here in the chat for me to see. Don't touch any files yet.

Let the table fill up as much as possible first (feel free to run /context to see how full it is), then move on.

Once you're ready, follow these steps and watch how the Context Window changes:

  1. Type /compact Only keep the current page's features and what's still unfinished
  2. Type /context again and compare: did the table usage go down?
  3. Ask it "What were the five improvement suggestions you just gave me?" and see how much it remembers
  4. Then ask "What features does the current page have?" It should be able to answer this one
  5. Type /clear, then ask "What features does the current page have?" again

Step 3 lets you see for yourself that compression is "lossy". In /compact we only told it to keep the features and unfinished work, not those five suggestions, so it'll likely miss some or give an incomplete answer.

Steps 4 and 5 are the same question asked twice. Before /clear, it can answer, because that's exactly the key point we told it to keep. After /clear, it can't even answer that one and has to re-read the file, because the table has been wiped.

Note

It's possible it still answers correctly, because the AI has an Auto Memory feature that decides on its own what's important and saves it automatically.

Wrap up

Quick recap of the key points:

  • Tokens are like LEGO bricks, and the Context Window is a table with a size limit, covered with your conversation with the AI, the files it read, command outputs, and so on.
  • The AI getting dumber over time isn't necessarily about lack of compute or the model getting worse. The table may just be too full, and the important stuff got compressed and diluted.
  • /context to look at the table, /clear to clear it, /compact to tidy it.
  • Core rule of thumb: one task, one conversation, and use /compact [what to keep] to hold on to the key points.

That's about it for today. See you tomorrow.
